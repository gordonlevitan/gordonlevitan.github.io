## Research

I am a PhD student in the [Gnaim group](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/MCMS/gnaim/) at the Weizmann Institute of Science. I use electricity in place of chemical reductants and oxidants to build organic molecules, and I study how the electrode–electrolyte interface decides which product forms.

### The electrolyte as a selectivity switch

In electro-reductive olefin coupling, the supporting electrolyte is usually chosen for conductivity and then ignored. In my first-author paper in *JACS* (2026), we show that it decides where the reaction happens and which product forms. With the same substrate, electrodes and solvent, changing only the electrolyte cation moves styrene hydrodimerization from exclusively linear to exclusively branched coupling.

{% include reaction-scheme.html %}

- **Ammonium salts.** Bu₄NBF₄ acts as both electrolyte and proton source. The radical adds to a second olefin in solution, giving tail-to-tail coupling.
- **Lithium salts.** Lithium deposits a Li-rich interphase on the electrode, where benzylic radicals couple head-to-head.
- **Evidence and scope.** Radical-clock probes, cyclic voltammetry, SEM, solid-state NMR, EPR and DFT. The approach extends to olefin cross-coupling and polar hydrofunctionalization.
